Below the line of visibility

Everyone is watching the front stage. We built the backstage.

AI keeps getting better at answers, and the reasoning behind them gets lost. Firms face the same problem: the evidence behind a decision is scattered, and the path from that evidence to the decision is never recorded.

OmnIA records that path: the events, the evidence and the approvals behind each decision, in logical and temporal order.

For firms that run on informed human judgment: law, consulting, family offices and others. The system connects the evidence and proposes paths. People who see the whole picture tie the threads together; people with authority decide. Here, AI and people each take their proper place.

The problem

Firms keep documents. They lose the reasoning.

Every decision should rest on evidence: the documents on file, what the organization learned before, and the judgment of the people in the meeting room. The documents stay. The learning and the judgment leave with the people, and that is where knowledge management usually fails.

When a partner leaves, their experience leaves too. When a decision is questioned five years later, hardly anyone can faithfully reconstruct the evidence behind it, who weighed it, or what the context was at the time.

In complex work, someone always has to do the synthesis: bring together what many people and sources produced and turn it into a decision. As the volume and complexity of information keep growing, a synthesis that stays faithful to the documents and sources becomes almost impossible. That is what OmnIA is for.

Why now

direction · optimize stage

Everyone will work with agents. Who keeps what they learned?

Agents read, weigh and propose all the time, but rarely show the reasoning that led to a conclusion. When the session ends, the thread is gone. Each person gains an assistant; the organization keeps little it can reuse.

OmnIA builds the environment behind the work: a shared record where the knowledge produced by people and their agents is kept with its evidence, its authors and its approvals.

How did we get here?

What evidence led us to this conclusion? That question is where OmnIA shows its value: it traces the decision tree back to the start of the process.

Backstage, OmnIA supports the work that happens on stage: research, findings, insights, recommendations and connections. Without a record, someone has to rebuild the path by hand. With OmnIA, the path back exists before the question does: each block of knowledge records the person or agent that produced it, the sources it came from, and who accepted or refuted it.

Many hands

in design

One project, many minds, a single record.

In a consultancy, a project runs on several fronts: one team on the numbers, another on interviews, a third on regulation. OmnIA links what each front produced, shows where the readings diverge, and presents the possible paths to whoever has the authority to choose.

Each approval is recorded at its level. An analyst confirms a source. A manager accepts a reading. A partner signs the recommendation.

decision rights are part of the record · who may approve what is defined before the system goes live

Complex decisions

in development

Chain of custody for reasoning.

A complex decision rests on premises, evidence, precedents and answers to objections. OmnIA keeps that chain and shows where a premise has nothing underneath it, where an objection was never answered, where a key document is missing.

It does not judge the merits. It shows the structure so your team can judge it, whether it is a law firm’s thesis, a consultancy’s recommendation, or a family office’s decision resting on an assumption no one wrote down.

A system of record for decisions.

Git gave software a memory of every change and the reason for it. OmnIA does the same for decisions.

Companies have systems of record for money, customers and code. None for decisions.

The method has a lineage: contribution analysis (Mayne), realist evaluation (Pawson and Tilley), and a proprietary evidence-assessment protocol, tested in practice in a multi-product evaluation for an international organization. The core is the same in every field. From a holding to a law firm to a clinic, only the sources it reads change.
