The problem

Firms keep documents. They lose the reasoning.

Every serious decision draws on three things: the documents on file, what the organization learned before, and the judgment of the people in the room. The documents stay. The other two leave with the people.

When a partner moves on, the reasons go with them. When a decision is questioned five years later, nobody can show what it stood on, who weighed it, or what was known at the time.

Why now

direction · optimize stage

Everyone will have an agent. Who keeps what they learned?

Agents read, weigh and propose all day, and the reasoning is gone when the session ends. Each person gains an assistant. The organization gains nothing it can keep.

OmnIA is the shared record: one place where what people and their agents worked out is kept with its evidence, its author and its approval.

How it works

validating

Ask why, and the path back is already there.

Work moves forward: research, findings, insight, recommendation. The question arrives the other way: why did we decide this? Without a record, someone rebuilds the path by hand, if anyone still can. With OmnIA, the path back exists before the question.

It matters more now. AI proposes without showing how it got there. OmnIA records how each piece of knowledge was produced: by which person or agent, from which sources, and who accepted it.

Many hands

in design

Many hands, one record.

In a consultancy, the same question is worked in separate streams: one team on the numbers, another on interviews, a third on regulation. OmnIA links what each stream produced, shows where the readings disagree, and takes the choice to whoever has the authority to make it.

Each approval is recorded at its level. An analyst confirms a source. A manager accepts a reading. A partner signs the recommendation.

decision rights are part of the record · who may approve what is defined before the system goes live

For law firms

in development

Chain of custody for the reasoning behind a case.

A complex case rests on claims, evidence, precedents and answers to objections. OmnIA keeps that chain and shows where a claim has nothing underneath it, where an objection was never answered, where a key document is missing.

It does not judge the merits. It shows the structure, so your team can. The same applies to a consultancy’s recommendation, or a family office decision resting on an assumption no one wrote down.

Companies have systems of record for money, customers and code. None for decisions.

The method has a lineage: contribution analysis (Mayne), realist evaluation (Pawson and Tilley), and a four-layer evidence protocol (existence, quality, internal relation, strength) first used in a multi-product evaluation for an international organization. The core is the same in every field. What changes from a holding to a law firm to a clinic is the sources it reads.
